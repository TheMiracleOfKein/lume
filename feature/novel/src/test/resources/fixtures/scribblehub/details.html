Synopsis

Jadis has no idea how she died. One minute she's living her life as a young college student in Pennsylvania, the next she's hurtling through the unimaginable, incomprehensible void of the space between realities as a lost and detached soul. Luckily for her, the bored, and slightly deranged, god D finds her soul before it is mulched by the incomprehensible cosmos. After offering her a cookie to calm down from her existential nightmare, he explains that not only are gods and their respective afterlives real, but her disbelief in gods while living also means she doesn’t qualify to enter any god’s afterlife upon death. However, since D is in the mood for some mischief, he strikes a deal with her. Life in a fantasy world with RPG elements, along with a body built to match Jadis' slightly perverted wishes. Additionally, she gets a guaranteed spot in his afterlife. All that for the low price of spicing up the stagnant cyclical nature of a never-ending war between two stubborn gods and their respective followers on said magic-filled world. What disembodied soul could turn down an offer like that?

Except, once Jadis takes the deal, she finds that she has been transported to the middle of nowhere and the lazy jerk of a god forgot to check if any of his followers were around to greet Jadis. Now freshly reincarnated as a Nephilim, Jadis is all alone and all but naked in an abandoned dwarven village located firmly in demon infested territory. Not planning on dying again anytime soon, she is forced to take the only non-perverted class option given to her and unexpectedly receives a second body for it. How is Jadis going to explain that when she gets back to civilization? From there, the now pluralized Jadis tries to survive long enough to understand her new system and skills, looking forward to the day she finds civilization, hot baths, and hotter elves.

      Nothing at first glance does this story justice. It is an example of taking a tried and true template and using it to make an exceptionally good story. There are some portions where it feels like a wall of text for me, however, that just may be adult life and my lack of sleep catching up. I won't tell you if you'll like it or not, but I will tell you what to expect. 

      First— the grammar. Is. Perfect. The story flows well and it is easy to follow the story as you go along (which says a lot, coming from someone with a severe lack of sleep and concentration). I don't recall any spelling or grammatical mistakes, which says a lot, because it means I rode the boat so smoothly I either didn't notice it or was too engrossed to care. There is also a good, conscientious use of those box/table/cell thingies to make the system and notifications pop out.

      Second— the characters. Our main character is Jadis (Jey-Diss). She, for some reason, died, and we catch her in the rift between worlds, watching the cosmos and going insane from the utter scale of that which was not meant for mortal eyes, just there, until she wasn't. She then finds herself in a place that can only be described as the very essence of a living room facing a figure that can only be described as the very essence of an anthropomorphic figure. The god, D (Dee), explains that not only are gods and their respective afterlives real, and that in her disbelief of gods (or lack of anyone capable of proving otherwise), Jadis does not qualify to enter anygod's afterlife upon death, but that she, Jadis, is well and, well... dead. Which means back to cosmic existential crises. Unless— that is, Jadis can accomplish D's quest for her. D seeks chaos, a fresh change in a stalemate of a war on one of his worlds. In exchange, D offers, for one, to not throw Jadis back into the void, even better, he will make a new body for her, custom built according to her deepest, darkest desires. Should Jadis accomplish D's quest, Jadis will be given a second chance to live the life she's always wanted to live, on a world full of magic and adventure (and hot elves), plus, a sweet spot reserved for her in D's afterlife. Jadis, obviously, accepts, lest the story end right there, and is rickrolled by D in the middle of nowhere, leaving her to figure out how to survive the night— and the demon spawn of Samleos he courteously deigned to not inform Jadis about.       The writing, plot and worldbuilding is so good in this novel I find myself skimming the smut chapters just so I can get back to the story. It is very rare a 200+ chapter story keeps me reading till the end, but this one did.

      Some points:

      • The writing handles the main character having multiple bodies really well. I never found myself confused about what's going on. 
      • The author is very good at giving different characters their own distinct voices and personalities.
      • The author is good at writing banter and humor between characters. 
      • I really liked how the main character, originally from earth constantly makes references to earth culture and every time the surrounding characters point out how it makes no sense in their world. The reactions really help make the worldbuilding feel authentic. For the author: I would really like a few more conversations where Jadis discusses earth technology and the reactions of the the other characters and how the struggle to understand how it would work without magic. Was kind of glossed over. I feel like we're slowly going to get more of this with Sabina and her inventing.
      • The worldbuilding is done very well, and avoids info dumping.
      • The main character makes mistakes, and is punished for it. The main character isn't just failing upwards - she makes mistakes and people die because of it.
      • The writing often loops back to something mentioned or encountered many chapters ago in a convincing way that just helps the entire world feel consistent and logically. It feels like the author has got a very solid grasp on their world and the writing shows.
