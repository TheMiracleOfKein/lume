# Lume v0.9.8

Send downloaded chapters straight to another phone nearby, without the internet and without a server.

## New

- **Send to a nearby device.** In Downloads, tap the send button, choose whole titles or single chapters (the screen shows the total size), and tap **Send**. On the other phone, open Downloads → **Receive**: phones sending with Lume on the same Wi-Fi show up by themselves. If they don't (guest and office networks often block this), type the address the sender shows.
- **A code on both screens.** When the phones connect, both show the same six-digit code. The sender checks it and taps **Send**. The receiver sees who is sending, the titles, the number of chapters and the size, then taps **Accept** or **Decline**. Nothing is saved before you accept. Everything after pairing is encrypted with a key made for that connection only.
- **Chapters arrive as downloads.** Each chapter is checked against a checksum and saved like a normal download, in the receiver's own storage and format (folder or CBZ). Received titles join the library and read offline. Turn on **Send read marks too** and chapters you've read show as read on the other phone.
- **Picks up where it stopped.** If the connection drops, connect again: chapters that already arrived are skipped. Chapters the receiver already has are skipped too. So are titles whose source the receiver doesn't have.
- Lume listens for other phones only while the send screen is open.

## Not yet

QR codes, Wi-Fi Direct and the phone's own hotspot (for phones without a shared Wi-Fi) are planned for later. For now both phones need to be on the same network.

## Updating

Install it over v0.9.7 as usual. Your library, progress, bookmarks and downloads are kept. There are no database changes.

## Privacy

Sending works only inside your local network and never goes through the internet. While the send screen is open, the phone announces its device name on the local network. On Android 17, Lume asks for local network access the first time you open the send or receive screen. [PRIVACY.md](../../PRIVACY.md) lists it.

## For developers

- `data/nearby/NearbyChannel.kt`: length-prefixed frames (at most 4 MB). The pairing is: the receiver sends its name and the SHA-256 of a fresh P-256 key, the sender sends its key, then the receiver reveals its own. Keys come from HKDF-SHA256 over the ECDH secret, salted with the transcript, one per direction. Frames are AES-GCM with counter nonces. The six-digit code comes from the transcript hash.
- `data/nearby/NearbyProtocol.kt`: `Offer` (titles, chapters, sizes, the whole chapter list, read ids) and the `Answer` with skipped keys. Then per chapter `Head` (page sizes, 128 MB at most each), data frames of 256 KB, `Tail` (SHA-256) and `Ack`. `NearbySender` and `NearbyReceiver` are plain Kotlin and run on Dispatchers.IO; closing the socket stops them.
- `data/nearby/NearbyTransfer.kt`: builds the offer from download rows and the offline cache, and plans what to skip. `saveReceived` caches the title and its chapter list, adds it to the library and marks chapters read (only marks that are new), then calls `DownloadStore.saveReceivedChapter`, which checks every page's image header. `NearbyAdvertiser` and `NearbyDiscovery` wrap `NsdManager` (`registerServiceInfoCallback` on Android 14+, a serial `resolveService` queue before).
- `DownloadStore.downloadChapter` now ends in the shared `storeChapter` (CBZ packing, picked-folder publishing, the download row).
- Tests: `NearbyProtocolTest` runs a sender and a receiver over localhost sockets (full transfer with skips, decline, a broken connection keeping earlier chapters, a non-Lume client turned away).
- Device check: skipped for this release (no emulator run). The checklist is in [DEVICE_TESTING.md](../DEVICE_TESTING.md#v098-send-downloads-to-a-nearby-device).
